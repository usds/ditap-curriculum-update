# Activity: Signal or Noise? Evaluation RFIs with AI Insight

Purpose:  
This activity helps you practice evaluating and transforming a flawed, restrictive Request for Information (RFI) using commercial-first procurement principles, OMB Memorandum M-26-12 guidance, and the article [What a Good RFI Actually Looks Like](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-good-rfi-actually-looks-like-jaime-gracia-cukne/). You will use an AI-powered analytical partner to execute a practical, four-step workflow designed to diagnose, strip out restrictive language, and rebuild an RFI into an effective, market-facing document.

Why This Matters to You

In Sprint 3, you defined your product vision and success criteria. Now, you must communicate that need to industry without accidentally writing a disguised Statement of Work (SOW). Far too many RFIs are just 30-question lists that ask industry to design a system around a favored incumbent.

OMB Memorandum M-26-12 makes it clear: if agencies do not thoroughly explore the commercial market, the Senior Procurement Executive (SPE) must write a detailed justification for a non-commercial award. A strong, outcomes-focused RFI is how you avoid that administrative burden and attract modern, agile vendors.

## Instructions

#### **Step 1: Source a Live RFI (5 Minutes)**

1. Join your assigned breakout group.  
2. Open your group's copy of **"Activity\_ Signal or Noise\_ Evaluation RFIs with AI Insight"** to use as your note-taking guide.  
3. Navigate to **SAM.gov** and quickly find a current or recent RFI related to:  
   * Digital services  
   * IT modernization  
   * Tech platform support  
4. Download or copy the text of the RFI's background, problem statement, and vendor questions so you can paste them into your AI tool.  
5. Open your preferred approved AI tool (e.g., Lumen, Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini)

#### Step 2: Execute the 4-Step AI Workflow (15 Minutes)

Run the following four prompts sequentially. Copy and paste each prompt into your AI tool, making sure to insert your sourced RFI text and requirement details where indicated.

⚠️ **Pro-Tip:** The AI acts as your analytical partner, not a replacement for your judgment. Read its outputs critically during this exercise.

#### **The 4-Prompt Sequence**

* **Prompt 1: Diagnose the Draft**  
  "You are a federal acquisition SME reviewing an RFI under commercial-preference rules. I am pasting the text of a live RFI for \[insert requirement/title\]. Review this text and do three things: 1\. List every question or requirement that is a solution specification in disguise. 2\. Identify every question industry has likely already answered in prior RFIs. 3\. Flag any language that would lead a commercial vendor to conclude the RFI is written around an incumbent. Be direct. \[Paste RFI text here\]"  
    
* **Prompt 2: Rewrite the Problem Statement**  
  "Using the same RFI text, rewrite the problem statement and background so it: Describes the mission, end user, and the gap between them; Removes references to specific technologies or labor categories; Names the outcome the agency needs in operational terms; Stays under 400 words. The goal is to let a commercial vendor understand what success looks like without being told how to deliver it.”

* **Prompt 3: Generate Commercial-Availability Questions**  
  "Based on the rewritten problem statement, draft five commercial-availability questions that satisfy FAR 10.001(f). They must ask: 1\. If the need can be met by products/services customarily available in the commercial marketplace. 2\. What minimum modifications would make it commercial. 3\. Industry to identify any requirement language that unnecessarily excludes commercial solutions. 4\. For specific commercial examples (contracts/customers). 5\. What commercial pricing models and contract types industry uses for similar work. Write in plain language."  
    
* **Prompt 4: Stress-Test the Final RFI**  
  "Review the revised problem statement and the 5 new questions. Answer these three questions: 1\. If I am a new commercial vendor, do I have enough info to decide whether to respond? 2\. If I am the incumbent, does anything indicate the follow-on is mine to lose? 3\. Would an SPE writing an annual compliance report be able to cite this RFI as evidence of genuine commercial market research?"

#### Step 3: Evaluate and Prepare to Report Back (5 Minutes) 

As a group, review the AI’s final stress-test and document your observations.

* **The Stripping Test:** Did the AI successfully remove the restrictive, unneeded language from the original SAM.gov RFI?  
* **The Volume vs. Value Test:** How does the clear utility of the new 5 commercial-availability questions compare to the focus of the original RFI questions?  
* **The Human Edit:** What is one specific adjustment your group would make to the AI’s output to ensure it aligns with your agency's actual operating environment?

## **Class Debrief (15 Minutes)**

Be ready to select a spokesperson to share a 2-minute summary with the class:

1. The title/agency of the RFI you selected from SAM.gov.  
2. Your biggest "aha\!" moment regarding how restrictive or incumbent-centered the original RFI actually was.  
3. Whether your group agreed or disagreed with the AI's final Prompt 4 stress-test evaluation.

## Outcome

By the end of this activity, you’ll understand how to use AI tools as analytical partners to validate that your market research communications are clear, intentional, and designed to attract qualified, modern vendors. Specifically, you will know how to apply a practical, four-step workflow to actively diagnose, strip out restrictive language, and pivot your RFIs toward an outcomes-based, commercial-first framework that aligns with OMB Memorandum M-26-12 requirements
